A

The Loop

Inspired by Las Vegas Strip resorts: the racetrack aisle and open, high-ceilinged floor

Everything is in one big room. A ring aisle circles the pits and the slots line the outside, so you can see the whole floor from anywhere on the loop. The centre bar sits at the far end of the entrance aisle and pulls you across the floor. The side rooms open off the floor's walls: archways where the noise should carry (lounge, sportsbook), doors where it shouldn't (high limit, restaurant).

The Promenade

Inspired by the grand European casinos: an enfilade of salons, slots kept apart from the tables

You walk in a straight line from the lobby through the Grand Hall and the Grand Salon to the Salle Privée, and each doorway shows the next room only once you reach it. The tables stay on the axis while the slots get their own hall, with a terrace linking it back to the salon so there's a loop to wander. It has the fewest tables of the three and the most sense of arrival.

The Boulevard

Inspired by integrated resorts with indoor shopping streets, plus the Macau habit of separate baccarat salons

The spawn opens onto a street rather than the casino. The restaurant, show lounge, café, sportsbook and shop doors all open off it, so there's life before you place a bet. Three archways lead north onto a big floor with tight slot clusters at the front, then the pits round an island bar. The Vault, the Jade Room and the High Limit Slots open off its back and sides. It's the biggest of the three and has the most to wander through.

Building it in CasinoWorld

Doors or archways

Rooms joined by an archway draw together, so archways carry the floor's noise and bustle into the bar, lounge and sportsbook. A room behind a door stays hidden until you reach the doorway, which is right for the high limit rooms and the restaurant. They feel private, and each door gets a named tab on the minimap.

Lobby kept as is

All three plans build north of the live Modern lobby (608 × 400) and keep the two lifts. The only change is turning the lobby's 64 px top door into a 192 px archway, so you can see the casino from the spawn point.

NPCs-only rooms

Kitchen, staff, restrooms and the lifts are dead ends in the same way the lifts are now. NPCs arrive there and walk back in, so the restaurant gets waiters, the pits get dealers walking in for a shift change, and people pop off to the restrooms, with none of it costing any art.

Table count and performance

These plans have 13 to 25 tables. Every room is built on every client, and the architecture notes say that past a few dozen tables the server should only send each player the tables in rooms they can see. That cut-off is worth doing before the biggest of these goes live.

High limit tools you already have

Table limits and the limits sign set the high-limit rooms apart (the labels like "Blackjack £500" are only suggestions). A Master table gives a small salon every game without adding tables, and the cashier cage and ATMs tie in with the Bank.

Walking distances

At 105 px a second, the furthest rooms are about half a minute from spawn in every plan (route lengths from the server's own walk timer). If that feels long in play, shrink the main floor rather than the side rooms, or add a second spawn point nearer the action.

Mixing them

The parts swap between plans: A's racetrack aisle would fit inside C's floor, B's terrace loop could hang off A's slot side, and C's Jade Room works as a side room in any of them. Each room is its own blueprint, so a hybrid is mostly moving rooms around on the Floor plan tab.

What's a placeholder

Restaurant tables are round tables with armchairs, the sportsbook counter is reception desks, and every slot is set to Armadillo so the checker passes. The real art would come from the Vegas, Modern and Nocturne sets or new uploads.
